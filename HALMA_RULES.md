# Halma Rules

This summary is based on `halma_game.pdf`, a Martin Gardner article about Halma and Halma-style puzzles. Halma is a late-19th-century board game whose name comes from the Greek word for "leap". It is one of the ancestors of Chinese checkers.

## Goal

Move all of your counters from your starting yard into the opposing yard. The first player, or team in a partnership game, to occupy the required opposing yard with all of their counters wins.

## Board

Traditional Halma is played on a square board with 16 cells on each side.

## Setup

### Two Players

1. Use two contrasting sets of counters.
2. Each player starts with 19 counters.
3. Place one player's counters in the top-left yard.
4. Place the other player's counters in the bottom-right yard.
5. The two yards are opposite corner regions of the board.

### Four Players

1. Each player starts with 13 counters.
2. The four yards are placed at the four corners of the board.
3. Players may play individually, each trying to reach the diagonally opposite yard.
4. Players may also play in partnerships, with opposite or adjacent players helping each other. In partnership play, the first pair to yard all 26 of its counters wins.


## Turn Order

Players alternate turns. On each turn, a player moves exactly one of their own counters.

A turn may be either:

1. A single step.
2. A hop, including a connected chain of hops.

Steps and hops cannot be combined in the same turn.

## Legal Moves

### Step

A step moves one counter to any one empty adjoining cell, like a chess king.

On a square board, this means a counter may step one cell in any of the eight directions:

- Horizontally.
- Vertically.
- Diagonally.

### Hop

A hop moves one counter over an adjacent occupied cell and lands in the empty cell immediately beyond it.

Hop rules:

- A hop may be made in any horizontal, vertical, or diagonal direction.
- The jumped counter may belong to either player.
- Jumped counters are not removed from the board.
- A player is not required to make a hop just because one is available.

### Hop Chain

A connected chain of hops counts as one move.

Hop-chain rules:

- After one hop, the same counter may continue hopping if another legal hop is available.
- The player may continue the chain as long as possible, or stop at any point.
- A chain may jump over friendly and enemy counters in any mixture.
- A chain may change direction between hops.
- A hop chain is still a single turn.

## End State

The game ends when a player has moved all of their counters into the opposing yard. That player is the winner.

In a two-player game, a player wins by occupying the opponent's starting yard with all 19 counters.

In a four-player partnership game, a team wins when all 26 counters belonging to that pair have reached their target yards.
